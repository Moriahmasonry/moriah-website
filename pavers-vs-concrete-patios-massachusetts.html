Pavers vs. Concrete Patios in Massachusetts: How to Choose

Prepared for Moriah Masonry Works · Draft for company review · November 20, 2026

Choosing between concrete pavers and a poured concrete patio? Start with the look you want, the conditions of your yard, and how you would prefer to maintain and repair the surface over time.

The short answer

Concrete pavers are worth considering when patterns, borders, and access for localized repairs matter to you. Poured concrete may suit a simpler, continuous-surface design. For either option, compare the complete installation—not just the surface material—and ask how the project will address drainage, ground preparation, and winter exposure.

This guide compares conventional interlocking concrete pavers with a poured concrete slab. Natural stone, brick, stamped finishes, and engineered permeable systems can involve different requirements.

CMHA explains that interlocking pavers can be removed and reinstated for access to utilities or repairs to localized base deformation. That repairability is useful, but it does not make the pavement immune to settlement or maintenance needs. [1]

What matters during Massachusetts winters?

Winter exposure deserves a place in your patio discussion. For poured concrete exposed to freezing and thawing, mixture selection matters: the American Concrete Institute explains how intentionally entrained air improves resistance to freeze-thaw damage in saturated concrete. This is one part of durability, rather than a promise that a surface cannot deteriorate. [2]

Also ask how the concrete will be protected and cured after placement. Curing means managing moisture and temperature while concrete develops its properties; it is not simply waiting for the surface to look dry. [3]

For pavers, ask whether the selected product and installation system suit the exposure. CMHA’s maintenance guidance addresses freeze-thaw durability, joint condition, settlement, and repairs. Neither choice should be sold as maintenance-free. [4]

Look beneath the surface: base preparation and drainage

A useful patio proposal should explain how the finished surface will meet the house, steps, and surrounding yard—and where water will go. Ask what excavation and ground preparation are included, how the supporting layers will be constructed, and whether existing conditions require additional work.

Do not assume that gaps between pavers automatically make a patio permeable. Permeable interlocking concrete pavement is a specifically designed system with appropriate joints and permeable aggregate layers. Standard pavers and a permeable pavement system should not be treated as interchangeable. [5]

Massachusetts homeowner guidance identifies impervious surfaces as contributors to stormwater runoff. If reducing runoff is a priority, raise that goal before selecting the pavement system, so drainage can be considered as part of the design. [6]

How should you compare patio estimates?

A price per square foot cannot tell you whether two proposals include the same work. Before choosing, request a clear scope covering removal and disposal, excavation, preparation, the selected surface, drainage work, transitions, and site restoration. Identify optional items such as borders, steps, or connected walkways separately.

This guide does not quote a Massachusetts installation price. The useful next step is a project-specific estimate based on your address, proposed area, material preferences, and site conditions. Compare those details alongside the price.

What about cracks, repairs, and ongoing care?

Poured concrete can crack as it changes volume. Planned joints help control where cracking occurs, but they cannot eliminate all cracking. Ask how joints will be arranged and what the contractor recommends if a crack appears. [7]

With conventional interlocking pavers, maintenance may include replenishing joint material and correcting localized settlement after identifying the cause. Keeping spare matching units can help with later repairs. Follow product-specific cleaning and winter-care instructions rather than assuming every cleaner, sealer, or deicer is suitable. [4]

Five questions to ask before hiring a patio contractor

  1. What preparation is included, and what conditions could change the scope?
  2. How will the patio meet existing doors, steps, and walkways?
  3. Where will water go after rain or snowmelt?
  4. What maintenance, repair options, and written warranty terms apply?
  5. What can affect the schedule, and when can we begin using the patio?

Choose around your property and priorities

If individual patterns, contrasting borders, and access for localized repairs are priorities, discuss a paver patio. If you prefer a simpler continuous-surface design, discuss poured concrete. In either case, ask for an explanation of the complete system and the care it will need.

For homeowners in Greater Boston, MetroWest, the North Shore, and the South Shore, the most useful comparison starts with the actual property—not a blanket claim that one material is always better.
